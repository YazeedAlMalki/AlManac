    49	
    50	Reuse all of it unchanged. Modularity is preserved by keeping every module's
    51	records in its own tables and letting these serve all of them.
    52	
    53	| Component | File | Why it serves the whole application |
    54	|---|---|---|
    55	| `Database` | `Persistence/Database.swift` | A seam, not an ORM (`:43-50`). Plain SQL keeps schema knowledge in migrations, where the runner can enforce it. Foreign keys on (`:68`); `transaction` rolls back on throw (`:168-178`). |
    56	| `MigrationRunner` | `Persistence/MigrationRunner.swift` | Ordered, transactional, idempotent; refuses duplicate versions, out-of-order insertion (`:58-60`) and edited applied migrations (`:53-56`). |
    57	| `TimeModel` / `LogicalDay` | `Time/TimeModel.swift` | The single day-assignment rule. Boundary injected, `.midnight` a placeholder (`:16-33`). `bounds(of:)` adds a calendar day, not 86,400 seconds (`:78-82`). |
    58	| `Clock` | `Time/Clock.swift` | Injectable now, with `FixedClock` for tests. |
    59	| `SourceIdentifier` | `Provenance/SourceIdentifier.swift` | `namespace:localID`, never replaced by an internal key. Adding a namespace forces a licence classification at compile time (`:22-33`). |
    60	| `LicenceGroup` / `BundleGuard` | `Provenance/LicenceGroup.swift` | A failing build assertion for shipped reference data (`:42-49`). |
    61	| `SyncAnchorStore` | `Sync/SyncAnchorStore.swift` | Keyed by `domain` (`Migrations.swift:23-28`), so already generic. Failure preserves the last good anchor (`:59-67`). §9 changes how it is *used*, not what it stores. |
    62	| `BackupService` | `Backup/BackupService.swift` | Online-backup snapshot covers new tables for free (`:35-55`). `restore` throws by design (`:62-67`). |
    63	
    64	**Do not** add an ORM or a generic repository layer on top of `Database`. A
    65	shared foundation is exactly the change that invites one, and it would move
    66	schema knowledge out of migrations.

   113	## 5. Events, measurements, plans, derived summaries
   114	
   115	| | Definition | Mutability | Example |
   116	|---|---|---|---|
   117	| **Event** | Something that happened at a time or over an interval. | Correctable by revision, never silently overwritten. | A specimen collected; a bout performed; a meal eaten. |
   118	| **Measurement** | A qualified value attached to an event or subject. | Append-only; an amendment is a new revision. | A ferritin observation; a nutrient amount; an HR sample. |
   119	| **Plan** | A prescribed intention that may or may not be executed. | Has its own lifecycle. | An ordered panel; a prescribed 5×5; a protein target. |
   120	| **Derived summary** | Computed by a versioned rule from the above. | Droppable and rebuildable. | Daily macro totals; a readiness score; an eGFR Almanac computed. |
   121	
   122	Three rules, each with direct evidence:
   123	
   124	1. **A plan is never stored in the same row as its outcome** — Prescription
   125	   Model §1. An ordered panel that returned "insufficient specimen" is a plan
   126	   with no measurement and must stay visible as exactly that.
   127	2. **A measurement is never stored without its qualifying dimensions and its
   128	   preserved source text** — Processing Design §2; Laboratory §5.
   129	3. **A derived summary records the rule version and its inputs, and is never
   130	   the only copy of a fact** — Laboratory §6.2 is the concrete instance.

   134	## 6. Shared measurement storage versus shared interfaces
   135	
   136	v0.1 said a shared measurement model is information-destroying. That was
   137	wrong, and §13 records the correction. A shared model with enough dimensions —
   138	value type, comparator, derivation, missing reason, source text, unit class,
   139	precision — destroys nothing. What destroys information is an *under-specified*
   140	shared model: a bare `value REAL` with an untyped `metadata JSON` beside it.
   141	
   142	So the real question is not "shared or specialised" but **what would a shared
   143	model have to carry, and what does it cost to make every module pay for it.**
   144	
   145	| | One shared measurement table | Per-module tables |
   146	|---|---|---|
   147	| Dimensions | Must be the union of every module's: lab's five plus comparator plus ranges plus revision chain, nutrition's qualifier plus basis, exercise's type-dependent measured field. | Each module carries only its own. |
   148	| Constraints | Table-level constraints must hold for every module, so most become nullable and unenforced; per-module validity moves into application code. | `CHECK` and `NOT NULL` express each module's real rules. |
   149	| Foreign keys | Cannot point at module-specific parents (specimen, meal, bout) from one table without polymorphism. | Direct and enforced. |
   150	| Aggregation | No shared aggregation is meaningful across modules anyway; every query re-adds module knowledge in its predicates. | Each module aggregates its own. |
   151	| Schema change | A new module's dimension widens the shared table for all modules and rewrites one large table. | Additive, isolated. |
   152	| Cross-module queries | Single table scan. | Requires a union or per-module queries. |
   153	| Migration cost if reversed | Splitting a populated shared table per module is a data migration. | Merging later is also a data migration. |
   154	
   155	The deciding factor is not information loss. It is that the union of
   156	dimensions is large, mostly nullable outside its own module, and unenforceable
   157	at the table level — and the only benefit, one-table cross-module querying, is
   158	addressed by §7 without a shared payload table.
   159	
   160	**Chosen: per-module tables, with shared behaviour expressed as protocols.**

   164	## 7. The timeline — reassessment
   165	
   166	v0.1 proposed a persisted `health_event` index. This section reconsiders it
   167	against the alternative of module-provided queries.
   168	
   169	**Module-provided queries:** each module implements a protocol returning its
   170	own entries for a time range from its own tables; a thin coordinator merges the
   171	results and orders them. No shared table, no second copy of anything.
   172	
   173	**Persisted event index:** each module also writes a row into a shared index
   174	table; the coordinator queries that one table.
   175	
   176	| | Module-provided queries | Persisted event index |
   177	|---|---|---|
   178	| Second copy of data | None. | Yes — index rows duplicate identity and time. |
   179	| Consistency risk | None. The module's record is the only copy. | Dual write; index can drift on insert, update, delete or revision. |
   180	| Cross-module day view | N queries, merged in memory. | One indexed query. |
   181	| Paging across long history | Needs an N-way merge cursor. | Natural. |
   182	| Deletions and revisions | Automatic — the record is the truth. | Needs cascade rules and orphan repair. |
   183	| New shared table names | None. | Occupies a generic name the unavailable spec may want (§12). |
   184	| Cost to introduce later | Additive; buildable from module records at any time. | — |
   185	| Cost to remove later | — | Modules already write to it; removal is a migration. |
   186	
   187	**Chosen: module-provided queries. The index is deferred.**
   188	
   189	*Implemented in exactly this form:* `TimelineProviding` on `LabStore` and
   190	`HealthSampleStore`, merged by `Timeline` with a total order, and **no index
   191	table**. See `docs/implementation-status.md` §2C.
   192	
   193	The concrete reason: the index's only genuine advantage is query performance
   194	and paging ergonomics, and **there is no performance problem to solve** —
   195	there are no modules, no data, no UI, and no measurement showing that an
   196	N-way merge is too slow. Every other row favours the queries, and the
   197	asymmetry is decisive: the index can be added later from module records
   198	without changing a single module table, whereas removing it after modules
   199	write to it is a data migration. Choosing the reversible option costs nothing
   200	today.
   201	
   202	The trigger for revisiting, stated so the deferral is not indefinite: when a
   203	range view must merge more than four modules, or page across more than a year
   204	of history, **and** profiling shows the merge dominating.
   205	
   206	### 7.1 The protocol
   207	
   208	```swift
   209	public protocol TimelineProviding {
   210	    static var domain: String { get }
   211	    func entries(from: Date, to: Date, in db: Database) throws -> [TimelineEntry]
   212	}
   213	
   214	public struct TimelineEntry: Sendable {
   215	    public let domain: String
   216	    public let kind: String
   217	    public let recordTable: String
   218	    public let recordID: String
   219	    public let occurrence: OccurrenceTime      // §8 — precision and zone
   220	    public let title: String
   221	    public let detail: String?
   222	    public let value: ValuePresentation
   223	    public let lifecycle: String               // module's own vocabulary
   224	}
   225	
   226	public enum ValuePresentation: Sendable {
   227	    case none
   228	    case quantity(text: String, unit: String?)
   229	    case bounded(comparator: String, text: String, unit: String?)
   230	    case coded(text: String)
   231	    case narrative(text: String)
   232	    case ratio(text: String)
   233	    case titer(text: String)
   234	    case missing(reason: String)
   235	    case derived(text: String, ruleVersion: String)
   236	}
   237	```
   238	
   239	v0.1's `isEstimated: Bool` is removed. Missing, qualitative and bounded values
   240	are three different things and each has its own case; the module formats its
   241	own value and the timeline never reinterprets it.
   242	
   243	### 7.2 If the index is later adopted
   244	
   245	Requirements, so that the deferral does not become a trap:
   246	
   247	- **Fully rebuildable from domain records alone.** A maintenance routine must
   248	  be able to drop the table and repopulate it entirely from module data.
   249	- **Update, delete and revision coverage.** Each module exposes a
   250	  `changes(since:)` contract; the index is not maintained by hand-written
   251	  call sites.
   252	- **Orphan repair.** An index row whose target no longer exists is detected
   253	  and removed by a routine, not by a foreign key — SQLite cannot enforce one
   254	  against a polymorphic target.
   255	- **Staleness detection.** The table records the schema version and the day
   256	  boundary tag it was built under, so a boundary change or a schema change is
   257	  detectable rather than silent.
   258	- **A derived artefact, never a source.** Losing the index must never lose a
   259	  fact.

   263	## 8. Time, precision and zone
   264	
   265	Shared across every module. Laboratory §12 is the module-specific application.
   266	
   267	- Four distinct times, never interchangeable: **scheduled** (a plan's intended
   268	  time), **occurrence** (when the thing happened — collection, meal, bout,
   269	  sleep segment), **recording** (when it entered Almanac), and where a source
   270	  issues records, **reported** (when the source issued it).
   271	- Every stored time carries a **precision**: `instant` · `minute` · `hour` ·
   272	  `day` · `month` · `year` · `unknown`.
   273	- Every stored time carries an offset and, where known, a zone identifier, so
   274	  a record made in another zone is not reinterpreted in the device's current
   275	  one.
   276	- **No timestamp is ever fabricated to fill a field.** A date-only fact stays
   277	  at `day` precision.
   278	
   279	Day assignment:
   280	
   281	- Uses the occurrence time where present, else the reported time, else the
   282	  recording time, and **records which was used**.
   283	- Goes through `TimeModel` (`Time/TimeModel.swift`); no module formats a date.
   284	- At `day` precision or coarser with a non-midnight boundary, the assignment
   285	  is marked approximate. The boundary is still a placeholder
   286	  (`TimeModel.swift:16-20`), so this case is currently the common one and must
   287	  not be resolved by assuming a time.
   288	
   289	Interval overlap:
   290	
   291	- Defined only when both intervals have known start and end at `minute`
   292	  precision or finer with known offsets.
   293	- Otherwise the answer is **unknown**, which is a third value and not `false`.
   294	  Sleep stage overlap, fasting windows and workout merging all depend on this.
   295	
   296	Ordering:
   297	
   298	- Records at `minute` precision or finer order against each other.
   299	- Coarser records group as time-unknown within their day rather than sorting
   300	  against an invented time.

   304	## 9. Import jobs and synchronisation cursors
   305	
   306	v0.1 conflated these. They are different mechanisms with different failure
   307	modes.
   308	
   309	| | Import job | Sync cursor |
   310	|---|---|---|
   311	| Input | A document or file the user supplied. | A change stream the source exposes. |
   312	| Identity | The document. | The stream position. |
   313	| Progress record | Job row: status, counts, per-row outcomes, errors, document link. | Opaque anchor token per domain. |
   314	| Re-running | Idempotent by source identity; produces the same rows. | Resumes from the anchor. |
   315	| Failure | The job fails; the document remains; nothing is lost. | The anchor must not advance past unpersisted data. |
   316	| Existing code | None. | `Sync/SyncAnchorStore.swift`, `sync_anchor` table. |
   317	
   318	Two requirements follow.
   319	
   320	- **R9.1** File imports get their own job record. They do not use
   321	  `sync_anchor`, and a cursor is not invented for them.
   322	- **R9.2** For incremental sync, **the imported records and the cursor advance
   323	  commit in one transaction.** Today `SyncAnchorStore.save` is an independent
   324	  call (`Sync/SyncAnchorStore.swift:44`), so advancing the anchor without
   325	  persisting the rows it covers is expressible — and if it happened, the next
   326	  sync would start after data that was never stored, skipping it permanently.
   327	  `Database.transaction` (`Database.swift:168-178`) already supports the fix;
   328	  what is needed is an API shape that takes the row-writing work and the
   329	  advance together, so the unsafe ordering cannot be written.
   330	
   331	  This is a required interface change to `SyncAnchorStore`. It is **not made in
   332	  this revision** — no application code has been changed. The stored schema
   333	  does not change; only the call shape does.
   334	
   335	- **R9.3** `recordFailure` keeps its current behaviour: a failure never clears
   336	  the last good anchor (`:59-67`).

   340	## 10. Identity and external references
   341	
   342	Shared rules; Laboratory §9 is the module-specific application.
   343	
   344	- **R10.1** Every record has an Almanac-owned surrogate identifier. No natural
   345	  key serves as primary identity.
   346	- **R10.2** External identifiers are **scoped to their issuing source**.
   347	  Uniqueness applies to `(source_system, external_id)`, never to `external_id`
   348	  alone. Two sources may legitimately use the same string.
   349	- **R10.3** Repeated observations of the same thing are preserved as distinct
   350	  records. Nothing is deduplicated on the assumption that a repeat is an error.
   351	- **R10.4** Revisions form a chain: a new record referencing the one it
   352	  replaces, with a reason. The replaced record is retained and marked
   353	  superseded. Nothing is overwritten in place.
   354	- **R10.5** When idempotency cannot be established, the import creates a new
   355	  record and marks it as a possible duplicate for review. Silent merging is
   356	  prohibited.
   357	
   358	This applies equally to HealthKit samples: `HealthChangeSet.deletedExternalIDs`
   359	(`Health/HealthProvider.swift:48`) deletes by external identifier, and that
   360	deletion must be scoped to the providing source.

   364	## 11. Migration numbering
   365	
   366	### 11.1 What is actually applied
   367	
   368	Nothing. Per §1: one migration is *declared* (version 1, `core_infrastructure`),
   369	and no durable database exists in the repository, in `.build/`, or at any path
   370	configured in source. Tests build in-memory or UUID-named temporary databases.
   371	
   372	Consequences:
   373	
   374	- **Preserving applied migrations is satisfied trivially** — there are none.
   375	- **Migration 001 is preserved as declared anyway.** Its version and `name`
   376	  stay exactly as they are. It is referenced by `AlmanacMigrations.all`
   377	  (`Migrations.swift:50-56`), by the tests, and by `README.md`; changing it
   378	  buys nothing and would invalidate the runner's drift check the moment a
   379	  database exists.
   380	- **Numbering is not yet frozen, and freezes the moment an app target creates
   381	  its first durable database.** That is the deadline, and it is worth writing
   382	  down because it will arrive without an announcement.
   383	
   384	### 11.2 The sequence
   385	
   386	| | Number new work 002 upward now | Reserve a block for the spec's schema |
   387	|---|---|---|
   388	| Requires knowing how many migrations the spec needs | No | Yes — which is inference about an unavailable document |
   389	| If the spec's schema must land first | Renumber the not-yet-applied local migrations, free while nothing is applied | No renumber |
   390	| If the reserved block is the wrong size | — | Either wasted numbers or the same renumber anyway |
   391	| Risk after the first durable database exists | Renumber is no longer free; the fix becomes a new higher migration | Same |
   392	
   393	**Recommended sequence:** keep `001 core_infrastructure` unchanged; number new
   394	module migrations `002` upward contiguously as they are authored; and treat
   395	renumbering as available until the first durable database is created. Record
   396	the freeze point in `README.md` when the app target lands.
   397	
   398	*Taken:* migration 001 `core_infrastructure` unchanged; 002
   399	`laboratory_catalog`, 003 `laboratory_records`, 004 `health_samples`. The
   400	target-database check that preceded this, and how far its evidence reaches,
   401	is recorded in `docs/implementation-status.md` §4.
   402	
   403	### 11.3 Correcting v0.1 on recoverability
   404	
   405	v0.1 said applying a migration above the spec's reserved number would make
   406	that number "unrecoverable". That is wrong. `MigrationError.outOfOrder`
   407	(`MigrationRunner.swift:58-60`) is a policy in code, and the situation has at
   408	least three exits: renumber the pending migration above the applied head; ship
   409	the same schema change as a new higher-numbered migration; or, before any
   410	release, reset development databases. The real costs are narrower and worth
   411	stating accurately — installs that applied different orders can diverge, and
   412	after field installs a correction must be a new migration rather than an edit.

   416	## 12. Does the missing Technical Spec create schema conflicts beyond numbering?
   417	
   418	Yes — three, all semantic rather than mechanical. None of them is a numbering
   419	problem, and numbering is the least of them.
   420	
   421	1. **Duplicate definition of the same concept.** If the spec's 48 tables
   422	   already define laboratory, sleep or timeline structures, building them now
   423	   creates two homes for one fact. Reconciling that later is a data migration,
   424	   not a rename.
   425	2. **Name occupation.** A 48-table schema plausibly wants generic names —
   426	   `event`, `observation`, `measurement`, `sample`, `source`, `document`. Any
   427	   taken now forces the transcription to rename as it goes, and renaming
   428	   during transcription is exactly where transcription errors enter.
   429	3. **Contradicting invariants.** The spec defines the day-boundary rule, the
   430	   readiness definition and the workout merge model. A module that hard-codes
   431	   an assumption about any of them will disagree with the transcription
   432	   silently rather than loudly.
   433	
   434	Mitigations, all cheap and all adopted by this proposal:
   435	
   436	- Prefix every new table with its module (`lab_*`). Claim no generic name.
   437	- Introduce no new shared table at all — which §7 already concluded on other
   438	  grounds, and which removes conflict class 2 for the timeline entirely.
   439	- Hard-code no boundary, readiness or merge assumption; take them from
   440	  injected configuration.
   441	- Keep everything additive, so the transcription adds tables rather than
   442	  reconciling them.
   443	
   444	One scoping observation worth stating: blood-test tracking entered Almanac's
   445	scope on 2026-09-08, and the spec is dated 2026-08-05. It is therefore
   446	**likely, though unverified, that Laboratory is not among the 48 tables** — in
   447	which case conflict class 1 does not apply to this module. That is a
   448	probability, not a finding, and it is listed as an open question in §15.

   452	## 13. Corrections to v0.1
   453	
   454	| v0.1 said | Correction |
   455	|---|---|
   456	| Only results with matching measured/calculated status are comparable. | Matching derivation status does **not** establish comparability. Analyte identity, method, assay, unit and reference frame must also agree, and often cannot be determined from the report. Almanac surfaces the differences rather than asserting comparability. Laboratory §15.1. |
   457	| Unit conversion is analyte-specific because it needs molar mass. | Only mass↔substance-amount conversion needs molar mass. Decimal scaling within one dimension is general, and dimensionless values need nothing. Laboratory §8. |
   458	| A generic value column is "the coercion promoted to an architecture"; a shared measurement model destroys information. | A sufficiently dimensioned shared model destroys nothing. The objection is to *under-specified* shared storage. Per-module tables are chosen for constraint enforcement, foreign keys and isolated schema change — §6. |
   459	| Applying a migration above the reserved number makes it permanently unapplicable. | The refusal is a recoverable policy. The real costs are divergence between installs and needing a new migration rather than an edit after release. §11.3. |
   460	| Almanac does not interpret results, ever. | Interpretation is deferred for v1, not permanently prohibited. The data model supports adding it, provided interpretation is stored separately from source facts, versioned and attributable. Laboratory §18.2. |
